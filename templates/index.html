Tax Registration
Visit the Taxpayer Registration desk at any IRD office with a valid photo ID and proof of address, and complete the TIN application form. Once processed, you'll receive your unique Tax Identification Number, which you'll use for all future filings and payments.
Individuals typically need a valid passport or national ID and proof of address. Businesses additionally need a certificate of incorporation, articles of association, and details of directors/owners. The Helpdesk can confirm exactly what's needed for your situation.
Businesses register by submitting their certificate of incorporation, business details, and owner/director identification to the IRD, along with a completed business registration form. This registers the business for the relevant tax types, such as Corporation Tax and VAT.
Filing Taxes
Tax returns can be filed in person at an IRD office or, where available, through the online filing portal. Gather your income and expense records, complete the correct return form for your taxpayer type, and submit before the statutory deadline.
Annual income tax returns are generally due early in the calendar year following the tax year, while VAT and PAYE have monthly filing deadlines. Because deadlines can be adjusted, please confirm the current dates with the Helpdesk or check official IRD notices.
The form you need depends on your taxpayer type: individuals typically use the Individual Income Tax return, businesses use the Corporation Tax return, and registered businesses also file VAT and PAYE returns. TESSA or the Helpdesk can point you to the right one.
Payments
Payments can be made in person at the IRD cashier by cash, cheque, or card, and through any other electronic methods the IRD currently offers. Always keep your payment receipt as proof of payment.
Payments are accepted at the cashier of any IRD office, including the head office on Young Street, St. George's. See the "IRD Offices" section below for the full list of locations, hours, and directions.
General IRD Services
IRD offices are generally open Monday to Friday, 8:00 AM – 4:00 PM, excluding public holidays. See the "IRD Offices" section for hours at each specific location.
You can call the Helpdesk at +1 (473) 440-3556, email helpdesk@ird.gov.gd, or visit an office in person during business hours. TESSA can also answer general questions any time.
The IRD handles taxpayer registration, income tax, corporation tax, VAT and PAYE filing and assessment, payment processing, tax clearance certificates, and general taxpayer support and guidance.

📖 Tax Glossary

Plain-language explanations of common tax terms - no tax background needed.

TIN

Tax Identification Number

A unique number the IRD assigns to every registered taxpayer, used to identify you on all tax filings and payments.

Example: You'll write your TIN on every tax return and payment slip you submit.
VAT

Value Added Tax

A consumption tax added to the price of most goods and services at each stage of sale, ultimately paid by the end consumer.

Example: If VAT is 15%, a $100 item costs the customer $115 at checkout.
IT

Income Tax

A tax charged on the income individuals earn, such as wages, salaries, and self-employment earnings.

Example: A portion of your annual salary is paid to the IRD as income tax.
CT

Corporation Tax

A tax charged on the profits that registered companies and corporations make during their financial year.

Example: A company that earns a profit files a Corporation Tax return each year.
PAYE

Pay As You Earn

A system where employers deduct income tax directly from an employee's salary before paying wages, then remit it to the IRD.

Example: Your payslip shows "PAYE deducted" as a line item reducing your take-home pay.
👤

Taxpayer

Any individual or business that is registered with, and legally required to pay tax to, the IRD.

Example: Once you register and receive a TIN, you're officially a taxpayer.
📋

Assessment

The IRD's official calculation of how much tax you owe, based on the information in your filed return.

Example: After you file, the IRD sends an assessment confirming the amount due or refundable.
📤

Filing

The act of formally submitting your completed tax return to the IRD, whether in person or online.

Example: "Filing your return" means handing in your completed income tax form by the deadline.
−

Deduction

An allowable expense or amount subtracted from your income before tax is calculated, which can lower the tax you owe.

Example: Certain approved expenses can be claimed as deductions, reducing your taxable income.

🏛️ About the Inland Revenue Division

Grenada's Inland Revenue Division (IRD) operates under the Ministry of Finance and is responsible for administering and collecting the country's domestic taxes.

What We Do

The IRD registers taxpayers, processes tax returns and payments, issues Tax Identification Numbers (TINs) and Tax Clearance Certificates, and enforces Grenada's tax laws - covering income tax, GCT, property tax, stamp tax, and PAYE.

Why Taxes Matter

Tax revenue funds public services that residents rely on every day - roads, healthcare, education, security, and public administration. Voluntary compliance keeps the system fair for everyone.

Taxpayer Rights

Taxpayers are entitled to courteous, professional service; clear explanations of tax obligations; confidentiality of their financial information; and a formal process to object to an assessment they believe is incorrect.

Taxpayer Responsibilities

Taxpayers are expected to register when required, file accurate returns on time, keep supporting records, and pay amounts owed by the applicable deadlines.

Digital Transformation

The IRD continues to expand online services through the G-TAX portal - covering registration, e-filing, and e-payment - to reduce the need for in-person office visits. TESSA is part of that ongoing digital transformation.
